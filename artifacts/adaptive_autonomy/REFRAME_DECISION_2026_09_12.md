# Reframe Decision — 2026-09-12

## Decision

Rename the current research focus from **Online Interaction Policy Learning** to the working title:

**Adaptive Autonomy / Human Control-Burden Tradeoff**

This is a scope correction, not a claim of novelty.

## Retired / narrowed claims

The branch will not claim novelty merely for demonstrating:

- online preference learning;
- persistent user-specific memory;
- context-sensitive preference transfer;
- preference selectivity / exception handling;
- personalization-driven user-effort reduction;
- over-personalization risk;
- personalized safety concerns;
- generic ASK-vs-ACT decision making.

These are treated as related-work-covered components or established research directions.

## Primary falsification target

Test whether increasing adaptive autonomy can produce a **practically meaningful reduction in explicit user operating burden** while simultaneously preserving:

1. trajectory quality;
2. context-sensitive non-overreach;
3. epistemic boundaries;
4. authorization / irreversibility boundaries;
5. performance beyond a materially simpler static heuristic comparator.

A positive result requires the benefit to survive the already planned state-ablation controls; otherwise the adaptive state is not credited causally.

## Interpretation boundary

Even a positive bounded N-of-1 result would not establish:

- ordinary-user safety;
- a new personalization mechanism;
- product readiness;
- general population benefit;
- causal explanation of the existing long-running Jerry-AI interaction.

It would establish only that a bounded adaptive system occupied a measured lower-control-burden region without crossing the frozen integrity gates under this protocol.

## Why continue

The experiment remains useful because the outcome can distinguish among:

- safe reduction of explicit control burden;
- adaptation with no practical benefit;
- burden reduction purchased by overreach;
- no advantage over static heuristics;
- dependence on active human correction as part of the control loop.

The last outcome is especially relevant to the motivating product question: whether low-friction long-term AI coordination can be made usable by ordinary users without requiring them to become expert prompt engineers or constant error-correctors.
